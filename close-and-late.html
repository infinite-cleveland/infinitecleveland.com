Infinite Cleveland

Close & Late: The Clutch Spectrum

When the game is close and the innings are late - 7th inning or later, within 3 runs - the Cleveland lineup stops being what it is on paper and becomes something else entirely.

Jim Thome bats .252 across 69 timelines. He hits home runs that leave the bat at 175 mph. He is, by any reasonable assessment, one of the most dangerous hitters in either lineup.

In close-and-late situations, Jim Thome bats .137. Fifty-four plate appearances. Seven hits. Seventeen strikeouts. Two RBI. A strikeout rate of 31.5%. He is, by any reasonable assessment, a liability.

Omar Vizquel bats .316 across 69 timelines. He is a light-hitting shortstop whose job is defense and contact. He has 1 home run in 364 plate appearances. He is not supposed to be the hero.

In close-and-late situations, Omar Vizquel bats .553. Forty-seven plate appearances. Twenty-one hits. Five strikeouts. He reaches base in close-and-late more often than he makes an out. The shortstop becomes the best hitter in baseball when the game is on the line.

The gap between them - .137 and .553 - is not a normal spread. It is a chasm. It is two players occupying the same lineup, playing the same game, on opposite sides of a mirror that only appears after the 7th inning stretch.

The Three Faces of Cleveland's Late Innings

Omar Vizquel: The Ghost Who Can't Miss

Omar Vizquel @ baseball-reference.com

In 47 close-and-late plate appearances across 69 timelines:

  • 17 singles
  • 3 doubles
  • 1 home run
  • 9 walks
  • 5 strikeouts
  • 6 RBI

He reaches base 31 of 47 times. A .660 on-base percentage when the game is tight. This is not a real number. This is a number that belongs to a create-a-player in a video game that someone set to 99 in every clutch attribute.

In Timeline 27, the game tied 2-2 in the bottom of the 9th, Vizquel singles off Robb Nen with runners on first and third to walk it off. In Timeline 14, down 2 in the 8th with runners on second and third, he singles in both. In Timeline 68 - the 25-24 Apocalypse Game - he singles with the bases loaded in the bottom of the 9th, part of an 8-run rally that nearly saves Cleveland. In Timeline 49, bases loaded in the 7th, he singles in a run to extend the lead.

He does this everywhere. Every timeline. Every late-inning situation. The pitcher delivers the ball and Vizquel puts it in play and gets on base. Over and over. The multiverse does not contain a version of the 7th, 8th, or 9th inning where Omar Vizquel can be reliably retired.

Matt Williams: The Finisher

Matt Williams @ baseball-reference.com

In 51 close-and-late plate appearances:

  • 8 singles
  • 2 doubles
  • 5 home runs
  • 6 walks
  • 3 strikeouts
  • 14 RBI

Five home runs. Fourteen RBI. Three strikeouts. A 5.9% strikeout rate in late-and-close, against a 15.1% rate overall. Matt Williams does not merely maintain his performance when the game tightens - he improves. He becomes harder to strike out. He hits for more power. And most importantly, he hits with runners on.

Every single one of his 5 close-and-late home runs comes with runners on base:

  • Timeline 1, 8th inning: Down 1, runner on second. Williams hits a 2-run homer to give CLE the lead. They win 15-14.
  • Timeline 14, 12th inning: Tied, runner on second. Williams hits a 2-run walk-off homer. CLE wins 10-8.
  • Timeline 32, 10th inning: Down 2, solo homer. Not enough - FLA wins 5-4. Even Williams can't save every timeline.
  • Timeline 34, 7th inning: Up 3, solo shot. Insurance. CLE leads 6-1. (FLA comes back to win 7-6 anyway, because Timeline 34 is chaos.)
  • Timeline 69, 10th inning: Down 2, runners on first and second. Williams hits a 3-run walk-off homer. CLE wins 6-5.

Three of those five home runs are walk-offs. Three of five. When the game goes past 9 innings, Matt Williams ends it with a swing.

Jim Thome: The Disappearance

Jim Thome @ baseball-reference.com

In 54 close-and-late plate appearances:

  • 6 singles
  • 1 double
  • 0 home runs
  • 2 walks
  • 17 strikeouts
  • 16 flyouts
  • 11 groundouts
  • 2 RBI

Zero home runs. The man who hits baseballs at 175 mph, who has 10 home runs across 69 timelines including bombs off Leiter and everyone else - when the game tightens to within 3 runs in the 7th inning or later, he cannot leave the park. Not once. Fifty-four tries. Zero.

And the strikeouts. Seventeen in 54 plate appearances. Let the contexts speak:

  • Timeline 6, 8th inning, down 1, runners on second and third: strikeout.
  • Timeline 9, 7th inning, up 1, runners on first and third: strikeout.
  • Timeline 32, 9th inning, tied, no one on: strikeout.
  • Timeline 53, 9th inning, tied, runner on second: strikeout.
  • Timeline 62, 7th inning, up 2, runners on first and third: strikeout.
  • Timeline 67, 9th inning, down 1, runners on second and third: strikeout to end the game.

That last one - Timeline 67, the 23-22 game - Thome strikes out with the tying run on third base. It is the last at-bat of the game. Cleveland loses by 1. In the first inning of that same game, Thome hit a grand slam off Al Leiter at 173 mph.

The first inning and the ninth inning might as well be different sports for Jim Thome. In one, he is a god. In the other, he is swinging at air.
